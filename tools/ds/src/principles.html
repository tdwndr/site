The five

What they look like in practice

IPrinciple one

Delight lives in details, not features

I’m obsessive about small things, but mostly the ones people will actually feel: the first screen, the thing they do every day, the sentence they read twice.

  • Choose where the care goes.Before starting, not after.
  • Finish the small stuff.Curly quotes, a true minus sign, figures that line up.
  • Remember the in-between moments.Empty, loading and error states count too.
IIPrinciple two

Have an opinion, then defend it

Neutral is a choice too. On the new Lloyds app we ripped up precedents for decision-making and committed to deciding in the room. I like work that does the same: picks a direction, says why, and changes its mind when the evidence does.

  • Lead with a recommendation.Rather than a menu of equals.
  • Let headings say something.“Sessions are up 103%”, not “Sessions”.
  • Make the next step obvious.One thing stands out.
IIIPrinciple three

Plain language wins

“Too often we make things overcomplicated.” If someone has to read a sentence twice, I’ve made them do my job.

  • Words first.Then the design around them.
  • Specific beats woolly.If anyone could have written it, it’s not saying much.
  • A word over an icon,unless everyone knows the icon.
IVPrinciple four

Space is not wasted

I never want anyone to feel overwhelmed by something I’ve made. Room around things is how I make sure of it.

  • Start generous.Tighten when the job needs it, like a busy app.
  • Let space do the separating.Before lines and boxes.
  • Give the main thing room.So it’s obviously the main thing.
VPrinciple five

Own it

If it’s mine, it carries my fingerprints and my name. I’d rather something felt particular than looked like a default.

  • One recognisable choice.Something that couldn’t have come from a template.
  • Signatures stay special.Used sparingly, they stay mine.
  • Sign the work.A mark, a byline, a personal note.

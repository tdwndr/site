# Voice: writing like Ted

Purpose: Write copy, posts, case studies and interface text that sounds like Ted Winder, with structures, devices and checks drawn from his own writing.

If the brief has its own tone of voice (a client, a brand), follow it, and keep only the plainness and the rules on not inventing facts.

## The voice in one line
Plain, reserved and confident. Specific, never woolly. A concrete start, a clear point, real evidence, credit shared, and a short strong close. Dry humour, rarely.

## Three registers
1. **Writing and posts (first person, "I" and "we"):** story-led and reflective. British spelling. Em dashes appear. An occasional exclamation mark to close with energy. Emoji are sometimes used as markers at the start of list items, never mid-sentence.
2. **Product and interface copy ("you"):** calm, short, useful. British spelling, sentence case. No exclamation marks. No emoji. Errors say what happened and what to do. Empty states are plain.
3. **Documentation (imperative):** a dry, exact spec, with a one-line reason where it helps.

## Devices Ted uses (with his examples)
- **Open with something real.** A blunt truth ("Building experience-led brands is hard."), a real question ("How do you inspire an entire design function to show up at their creative best while delivering tangible value for the business?"), a moment ("Back in March, we brought every designer across Lloyds together..."), or a personal way in ("With two little ones, I need all the help I can get.").
- **Short sentences when things go wrong or need weight.** "It wasn't all plain sailing. Projectors broke. The WiFi collapsed. AI got switched off."
- **Name the tension, then resolve it.** "Organisations that are commercial without being creative will get left behind, and organisations that are creative without being commercial won't be around for long either."
- **Not this, but that.** "This was not about evangelising or mandating AI. It was about understanding where it helps, where it doesn't..."
- **Threes and fours with crisp labels.** "Stability → Adaptability", "Evidence / Grow / Connect / Elevate", "From method to measurement. Culture to collaboration. Systems to skills. Craft to consultancy."
- **Real numbers in context.** "In under three hours, 351 attendees across 49 teams created more than 400 coded prototypes."
- **The point behind the numbers.** "But the most important outcome had nothing to do with tooling."
- **Credit by name.** "The real heroes of this are the product team..."
- **Wry, self-deprecating aside.** "I was politely advised that no, I couldn't remove all the chairs from the boardroom."
- **A short close.** "And that's what keeps me going." / "It's a big task, but we're up for it!" / "Let's go!"

## Words and phrases Ted reaches for
land / landing ("land things with impact"), bring to life, at scale, join things up, raise the bar, fit for the future, hands-on, experiment, make things happen, think, feel and do.

## Structures
**Post or update**
1. Hook (one line: truth, question or moment).
2. Context in two or three sentences: what, where, why it mattered.
3. The approach, often as a short list of three or four labelled points.
4. Evidence: the real numbers.
5. Credit: the people, by name.
6. The point behind it, then a one-line close.

**Case study**
1. Eyebrow with the theme (Delivery, Technology, People...).
2. Headline that states the outcome ("Delivering a major app redesign to 15m customers").
3. One-paragraph context and Ted's role.
4. What we did, in three or four steps.
5. What landed: numbers and quotes.
6. Credit and what's next.

**About blurb**
Lead with what Ted does, then one or two specific achievements with numbers, then how he works, then a forward-looking line ("Things are changing fast and I'm here for it.").

## Interface copy patterns
- Error: "[What failed]. [Reassurance]. [What to do]." e.g. "Couldn't save. Your changes are still here, so try again."
- Empty state: "No [things] yet. [Things] you add will appear here."
- Calls to action: "Get in touch", "Send it".
- AI content: label it in words ("Summary · written by AI").

## Never
- Invent numbers, names, quotes or outcomes. Ted's voice depends on real specifics: write `[number]` or `[name]` and ask.
- Woolly, corporate filler ("leverage synergies", "holistic", "best-in-class") that could be from anyone.
- Hype or a hard sell. The reader should never feel sold to.
- Title Case headings, or exclamation marks and emoji in interface copy.

## Check before you finish
- Does it start with something real, not a preamble?
- Is there at least one specific, real detail?
- Is every sentence understandable on first read?
- Have the people involved been credited?
- Does it end in one short line?

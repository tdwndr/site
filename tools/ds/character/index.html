# Character: does it feel like Ted?

Purpose: Calibrate the overall feel of anything you make for Ted, and test it before you hand it back.

## The identity in one breath
Particular, esoteric, classy. A Tokyo stationery shop (obsessive about small things) crossed with an independent bookshop (curated, personal). Ted admires classic typography, letterpress and Apple's simplicity, and dislikes trends for trends' sake.

Where it sits (from Ted's interview answers): quiet rather than loud, minimal rather than rich, classic rather than contemporary, precise rather than loose, firmly airy, a little playful, slightly cool in manner. Neutral on soft/sharp, machined/handmade and humble/assertive.

What drives the work, in his words: "Solving problems. Building things. Products, solutions, experiences. Working through the uncertainty to land things with impact."

## Translate traits into moves

- **Quiet:** paper and ink for most of the screen, the accent as a spot. Not colour blocks on content pages.
- **Minimal:** remove until it hurts, then add one considered detail. Not decorative shapes to fill space.
- **Classic:** Newsreader for quotes and essay headings, sentence case, real typographic marks. Not trendy display fonts or all-caps headlines.
- **Precise:** aligned figures, spacing tokens, a strict left edge. Not one-off margins.
- **Airy:** generous section spacing, about 70 characters per line. Not more cards squeezed above the fold.
- **A little playful:** one drawn underline or one wry line per piece, at most. Not jokes in errors or bouncy animation.
- **Curated:** three faces, one accent, a few components. Not a new variant for every case.

## Tone calibration
- Confident without swagger. State things plainly; don't hype.
- Generous: team work is "we", and contributors get named. Personal views are "I".
- Humour is dry and self-deprecating, e.g. "I was politely advised that no, I couldn't remove all the chairs from the boardroom." Never at the reader's expense; never in an error.
- Creative and commercial together: Ted treats both as non-negotiable, so show value as clearly as craft.

## The test
Before handing anything back, ask:
1. Could this have come from anyone? If yes, add one particular TW/ds choice.
2. Is anything here only because it's fashionable? Remove it.
3. Does it feel calm at first glance? If not, take something away.
4. Is the useful thing obvious before the delightful thing?
5. Would Ted be happy to put his name on it?

## Composition recipe for a signature moment
Warm paper, a lot of space, a Schibsted Grotesk headline with one drawn underline under a single word, a big Newsreader numeral in the accent, a short paragraph with one highlighter mark, optionally framed by registration marks. That's the whole recipe; don't add more.
